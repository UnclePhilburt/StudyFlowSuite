SUPPLY AND DEMAND - ECONOMICS STUDY GUIDE

Basic Economic Concepts:

Scarcity:
- Limited resources vs unlimited wants
- Fundamental economic problem
- Forces choices and trade-offs
- Applies to all economies

Opportunity Cost:
- Value of next best alternative given up
- What you sacrifice when making a choice
- Example: Going to college costs tuition PLUS lost wages from not working
- Key to rational decision-making

Market Economy:
- Economic decisions made by individuals and firms
- Prices determined by supply and demand
- Private ownership of resources
- Also called capitalism or free market

Demand:

Definition:
- Quantity of good or service consumers are willing and able to buy at various prices
- Must have both desire AND purchasing power

Law of Demand:
- As price increases, quantity demanded decreases
- As price decreases, quantity demanded increases
- Inverse relationship
- Ceteris paribus (all else equal)

Why Law of Demand Exists:

Substitution Effect:
- When price rises, consumers substitute cheaper alternatives
- Example: Buy chicken instead of expensive beef

Income Effect:
- When price rises, purchasing power decreases
- Can afford less of everything
- Real income declines

Law of Diminishing Marginal Utility:
- Each additional unit provides less satisfaction
- Willing to pay less for additional units
- Example: First slice of pizza tastes better than fifth

Demand Curve:
- Graphical representation
- Price on vertical axis (Y-axis)
- Quantity on horizontal axis (X-axis)
- Slopes downward (negative slope)
- Shows inverse relationship

Demand Schedule:
- Table showing price and quantity demanded
- Numerical representation
- Example:
  Price: $10, Quantity: 100
  Price: $8, Quantity: 150
  Price: $6, Quantity: 200

Market Demand:
- Sum of all individual demands
- Horizontal summation of demand curves
- Total quantity demanded at each price

Factors Shifting Demand Curve:

Income:
- Normal goods: demand increases when income increases
  * Example: restaurant meals, new cars
- Inferior goods: demand decreases when income increases
  * Example: ramen noodles, used clothing

Price of Related Goods:
- Substitutes: goods that replace each other
  * Example: Coke and Pepsi
  * If price of Coke rises, demand for Pepsi increases
- Complements: goods used together
  * Example: hot dogs and hot dog buns
  * If price of hot dogs rises, demand for buns decreases

Tastes and Preferences:
- Changes in consumer preferences
- Influenced by advertising, trends, seasons
- Example: Fidget spinners became popular

Expectations:
- Future price expectations
- If expect higher prices, buy more now
- Future income expectations
- If expect raise, increase current spending

Number of Buyers:
- More consumers = higher demand
- Population growth
- Example: Baby boom increased demand for baby products

Change in Demand vs Change in Quantity Demanded:

Change in Quantity Demanded:
- Movement ALONG demand curve
- Caused by price change only
- Same curve

Change in Demand:
- SHIFT of entire demand curve
- Caused by non-price factors
- New curve
- Increase: shift right
- Decrease: shift left

Supply:

Definition:
- Quantity of good or service producers are willing and able to produce at various prices
- Must be willing AND able

Law of Supply:
- As price increases, quantity supplied increases
- As price decreases, quantity supplied decreases
- Direct/positive relationship
- Ceteris paribus

Why Law of Supply Exists:

Profit Motive:
- Higher prices mean higher potential profits
- Incentive to produce more

Law of Increasing Costs:
- Producing more becomes increasingly expensive
- Need higher prices to cover rising costs
- Diminishing returns

More Producers Enter Market:
- High prices attract new producers
- Low prices cause producers to exit

Supply Curve:
- Graphical representation
- Price on vertical axis
- Quantity on horizontal axis
- Slopes upward (positive slope)
- Shows direct relationship

Supply Schedule:
- Table showing price and quantity supplied
- Example:
  Price: $10, Quantity: 200
  Price: $8, Quantity: 150
  Price: $6, Quantity: 100

Market Supply:
- Sum of all individual supplies
- Horizontal summation of supply curves
- Total quantity supplied at each price

Factors Shifting Supply Curve:

Input/Resource Prices:
- Costs of production (labor, materials, rent)
- Higher costs decrease supply (shift left)
- Lower costs increase supply (shift right)
- Example: Oil price rise increases production costs

Technology:
- Better technology increases productivity
- Lower costs per unit
- Increases supply (shift right)
- Example: Assembly line, computers

Taxes and Subsidies:
- Taxes: increase costs, decrease supply
- Subsidies: decrease costs, increase supply
- Example: Farm subsidies increase agricultural supply

Expectations:
- Future price expectations affect current supply
- Expect higher prices: withhold supply now
- Expect lower prices: increase supply now

Number of Sellers:
- More producers = higher supply
- New firms enter market
- Example: Success of iPhone led to more smartphone makers

Natural Conditions:
- Weather, natural disasters
- Especially affects agricultural supply
- Example: Drought decreases crop supply

Change in Supply vs Change in Quantity Supplied:

Change in Quantity Supplied:
- Movement ALONG supply curve
- Caused by price change only
- Same curve

Change in Supply:
- SHIFT of entire supply curve
- Caused by non-price factors
- New curve
- Increase: shift right
- Decrease: shift left

Market Equilibrium:

Definition:
- Point where supply equals demand
- Quantity supplied = quantity demanded
- Market clears
- No shortage, no surplus

Equilibrium Price:
- Price at which market clears
- Also called market-clearing price
- Balance point

Equilibrium Quantity:
- Quantity bought and sold at equilibrium
- Where supply and demand curves intersect

Finding Equilibrium:
- Graph supply and demand curves
- Intersection point is equilibrium
- Read price and quantity from axes

Market Disequilibrium:

Surplus (Excess Supply):
- Quantity supplied > quantity demanded
- Price above equilibrium
- Unsold goods accumulate
- Pressure to lower price
- Example: Unsold inventory, clearance sales

Shortage (Excess Demand):
- Quantity demanded > quantity supplied
- Price below equilibrium
- Not enough goods for all buyers
- Pressure to raise price
- Example: Concert tickets sold out, long lines

Price Adjustments:
- Markets naturally move toward equilibrium
- Invisible hand (Adam Smith)
- Surplus → price falls → equilibrium
- Shortage → price rises → equilibrium

Changes in Equilibrium:

Increase in Demand (shift right):
- Higher equilibrium price
- Higher equilibrium quantity
- Example: New study shows coffee is healthy

Decrease in Demand (shift left):
- Lower equilibrium price
- Lower equilibrium quantity
- Example: Health scare about product

Increase in Supply (shift right):
- Lower equilibrium price
- Higher equilibrium quantity
- Example: New technology reduces production costs

Decrease in Supply (shift left):
- Higher equilibrium price
- Lower equilibrium quantity
- Example: Hurricane destroys crops

Simultaneous Shifts:
- Both curves can shift at same time
- Effect on price and quantity depends on magnitudes
- Four possible combinations

Elasticity:

Price Elasticity of Demand (PED):
- Responsiveness of quantity demanded to price change
- Percentage change in quantity demanded ÷ percentage change in price

Elastic Demand (PED > 1):
- Quantity very responsive to price
- Price increase → large decrease in quantity
- Example: Luxury goods, goods with many substitutes

Inelastic Demand (PED < 1):
- Quantity not very responsive to price
- Price increase → small decrease in quantity
- Example: Necessities (insulin, gasoline)

Unit Elastic (PED = 1):
- Percentage changes equal

Factors Affecting Elasticity:

Availability of Substitutes:
- More substitutes = more elastic
- Example: Many brands of soda

Necessity vs Luxury:
- Necessities: inelastic
- Luxuries: elastic

Proportion of Income:
- Large portion of income: more elastic
- Small portion: more elastic
- Example: Car vs candy bar

Time Period:
- Short run: more inelastic
- Long run: more elastic
- Example: Can't immediately find substitute

Price Elasticity of Supply (PES):
- Responsiveness of quantity supplied to price change

Elastic Supply (PES > 1):
- Easy to increase production quickly
- Example: T-shirts, simple manufactured goods

Inelastic Supply (PES < 1):
- Difficult to increase production quickly
- Example: Oil, agricultural products

Price Controls:

Price Ceiling:
- Maximum legal price
- Set below equilibrium
- Creates shortage
- Example: Rent control

Effects:
- Quantity demanded > quantity supplied
- Reduced quality
- Black markets
- Discrimination
- Waiting lists

Price Floor:
- Minimum legal price
- Set above equilibrium
- Creates surplus
- Example: Minimum wage

Effects:
- Quantity supplied > quantity demanded
- Unemployment (for minimum wage)
- Wasted resources
- Illegal activity

Applications:

Consumer Surplus:
- Difference between willingness to pay and actual price
- Benefit to consumers
- Area below demand curve, above price

Producer Surplus:
- Difference between price received and willingness to sell
- Benefit to producers
- Area above supply curve, below price

Total Surplus:
- Consumer surplus + producer surplus
- Measure of market efficiency
- Maximized at equilibrium

Deadweight Loss:
- Loss of total surplus from market inefficiency
- Occurs with price controls, taxes
- Transactions that would benefit both sides don't happen
