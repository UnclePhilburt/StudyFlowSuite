NEWTON'S LAWS OF MOTION - PHYSICS STUDY GUIDE

Sir Isaac Newton (1643-1727):
- English physicist and mathematician
- Published "Principia Mathematica" in 1687
- Laid foundation for classical mechanics
- Also discovered calculus and studied optics

Newton's First Law - Law of Inertia:

Statement:
"An object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion stays in motion with the same speed and direction, unless acted upon by an unbalanced force."

Key Concepts:

Inertia:
- Resistance to change in motion
- Depends on mass
- Greater mass = greater inertia
- Explains why it's harder to push a car than a bicycle

Balanced Forces:
- Equal forces in opposite directions
- Net force = 0
- Object remains at rest or moves at constant velocity
- Example: Book sitting on table

Unbalanced Forces:
- Forces that don't cancel out
- Cause acceleration
- Change in motion
- Example: Pushing a shopping cart

Examples:
- Passengers lurch forward when car brakes suddenly
- Objects on dashboard slide forward during sudden stop
- Ball keeps rolling on smooth surface until friction stops it
- Seat belts needed to restrain passengers

Newton's Second Law - Force and Acceleration:

Formula:
F = ma
Where:
F = Force (Newtons, N)
m = mass (kilograms, kg)
a = acceleration (meters per second squared, m/s²)

Statement:
"The acceleration of an object depends on the mass of the object and the force applied."

Key Relationships:

Force and Acceleration:
- Directly proportional
- More force = more acceleration
- Example: Pushing harder makes cart move faster

Mass and Acceleration:
- Inversely proportional
- More mass = less acceleration (with same force)
- Example: Harder to accelerate a truck than a car

Net Force:
- Sum of all forces acting on object
- Fnet = F₁ + F₂ + F₃ + ...
- Direction matters (use + and - for opposite directions)

Weight:
- Force of gravity on an object
- W = mg
- Where g = 9.8 m/s² (acceleration due to gravity on Earth)

Examples:

Problem 1:
A 10 kg object accelerates at 5 m/s². What force is applied?
F = ma
F = 10 kg × 5 m/s²
F = 50 N

Problem 2:
A 100 N force is applied to a 25 kg object. What's the acceleration?
F = ma
100 N = 25 kg × a
a = 100 N / 25 kg
a = 4 m/s²

Newton's Third Law - Action-Reaction:

Statement:
"For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction."

Key Concepts:

Action-Reaction Pairs:
- Forces always occur in pairs
- Equal in magnitude
- Opposite in direction
- Act on different objects (crucial!)

Examples:

Walking:
- You push backward on ground (action)
- Ground pushes you forward (reaction)

Swimming:
- You push water backward (action)
- Water pushes you forward (reaction)

Rocket Propulsion:
- Rocket pushes gas downward/backward (action)
- Gas pushes rocket upward/forward (reaction)

Sitting in Chair:
- Your weight pushes down on chair (action)
- Chair pushes up on you (reaction)

Important Note:
- Action and reaction forces act on different objects
- They don't cancel each other out
- Each object experiences one force from the pair

Related Concepts:

Force:
- Push or pull
- Has magnitude and direction (vector)
- SI unit: Newton (N)
- 1 N = force needed to accelerate 1 kg at 1 m/s²

Mass:
- Amount of matter in object
- Measure of inertia
- Scalar quantity
- SI unit: kilogram (kg)
- Constant regardless of location

Weight:
- Force of gravity on mass
- Vector quantity
- Changes with location (gravity varies)
- SI unit: Newton (N)
- Weight on Moon = 1/6 of weight on Earth

Friction:
- Force that opposes motion
- Caused by surface interaction
- Always opposes direction of motion
- Types: static, kinetic, rolling, fluid

Static Friction:
- Prevents object from starting to move
- Must overcome to start motion
- Usually greater than kinetic friction

Kinetic Friction:
- Acts on moving objects
- Generally constant for given surfaces
- Less than static friction

Air Resistance:
- Type of fluid friction
- Opposes motion through air
- Increases with speed
- Depends on object's shape and size

Terminal Velocity:
- Maximum speed of falling object
- Reached when air resistance equals weight
- Net force = 0
- No more acceleration

Momentum:
- Related to Newton's laws
- p = mv (momentum = mass × velocity)
- Vector quantity
- SI unit: kg·m/s

Conservation of Momentum:
- Total momentum before collision = total momentum after
- Applies when no external forces
- Based on Newton's Third Law

Free Body Diagrams:
- Diagram showing all forces on object
- Arrows represent forces
- Length of arrow = magnitude of force
- Direction of arrow = direction of force
- Essential for analyzing forces

Normal Force:
- Perpendicular force from surface
- Reaction to object pressing on surface
- Often equal to weight (if on horizontal surface)
- Can be greater or less than weight on inclines

Applied Force:
- Force applied by person or object
- Can be at angles
- Resolved into components (horizontal and vertical)

Tension:
- Force transmitted through rope, string, cable
- Always pulls, never pushes
- Same throughout rope (if massless)

Gravity:
- Attractive force between masses
- Gives objects weight
- Acceleration due to gravity (g) = 9.8 m/s² on Earth
- Acts toward center of Earth

Real-World Applications:

Safety Features:
- Airbags: increase time of impact, reduce force
- Seatbelts: restrain passengers (First Law)
- Crumple zones: extend collision time, reduce force

Sports:
- Baseball: greater force = greater ball acceleration
- Basketball: action-reaction in jumping
- Swimming: push water to move forward

Transportation:
- Cars: engine force overcomes friction
- Airplanes: thrust overcomes air resistance
- Rockets: action-reaction in space

Common Misconceptions:

Misconception 1:
"Heavier objects fall faster"
- Actually: In vacuum, all objects fall at same rate
- Air resistance affects falling speeds
- Mass doesn't affect gravitational acceleration

Misconception 2:
"Force is needed to maintain motion"
- Actually: Force needed to change motion
- Constant velocity requires no net force
- Friction makes it seem like force is needed

Misconception 3:
"Action and reaction forces cancel out"
- Actually: They act on different objects
- Can't cancel because they're not on same object

Problem-Solving Steps:

1. Draw free body diagram
2. Identify all forces
3. Choose coordinate system
4. Break forces into components if needed
5. Apply F = ma in each direction
6. Solve for unknown

Units:

Force: Newton (N)
- 1 N = 1 kg·m/s²

Mass: kilogram (kg)

Acceleration: meters per second squared (m/s²)

Velocity: meters per second (m/s)

Distance: meters (m)

Time: seconds (s)
