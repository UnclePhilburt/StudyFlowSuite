THE PERIODIC TABLE - CHEMISTRY STUDY GUIDE

Organization of the Periodic Table:

Basic Structure:
- Elements arranged by increasing atomic number
- 7 horizontal rows called periods
- 18 vertical columns called groups or families
- Atomic number = number of protons in nucleus
- Dmitri Mendeleev created first periodic table (1869)

Reading an Element Box:
- Atomic number (top) - number of protons
- Element symbol (middle) - 1 or 2 letter abbreviation
- Element name (below symbol)
- Atomic mass (bottom) - protons + neutrons

Groups (Vertical Columns):

Group 1 - Alkali Metals:
- Lithium (Li), Sodium (Na), Potassium (K), etc.
- Highly reactive metals
- Soft, can be cut with knife
- React violently with water
- Form +1 ions
- One valence electron

Group 2 - Alkaline Earth Metals:
- Beryllium (Be), Magnesium (Mg), Calcium (Ca), etc.
- Reactive metals
- Form +2 ions
- Two valence electrons
- Less reactive than Group 1

Groups 3-12 - Transition Metals:
- Iron (Fe), Copper (Cu), Gold (Au), Silver (Ag), etc.
- Good conductors of heat and electricity
- High melting points
- Can form multiple oxidation states
- Often form colored compounds
- Many are used as catalysts

Group 17 - Halogens:
- Fluorine (F), Chlorine (Cl), Bromine (Br), Iodine (I)
- Highly reactive nonmetals
- Form -1 ions
- Seven valence electrons
- React with metals to form salts
- Exist as diatomic molecules (F2, Cl2, Br2, I2)

Group 18 - Noble Gases:
- Helium (He), Neon (Ne), Argon (Ar), Krypton (Kr), Xenon (Xe)
- Inert/unreactive
- Full outer electron shell (8 electrons, except He has 2)
- Colorless, odorless gases
- Don't readily form compounds

Periods (Horizontal Rows):
- Period number = number of electron shells
- Period 1: 2 elements (H, He)
- Period 2: 8 elements (Li through Ne)
- Period 3: 8 elements (Na through Ar)
- Periods 4-7: Longer periods with transition metals

Periodic Trends:

Atomic Radius:
- Increases down a group (more electron shells)
- Decreases across a period left to right (stronger nuclear pull)
- Largest atoms: bottom left of table
- Smallest atoms: top right of table

Ionization Energy:
- Energy needed to remove an electron
- Increases across a period (left to right)
- Decreases down a group
- Highest: top right (noble gases, fluorine)
- Lowest: bottom left (francium, cesium)

Electronegativity:
- Ability to attract electrons in a bond
- Increases across a period
- Decreases down a group
- Highest: Fluorine (4.0 on Pauling scale)
- Noble gases not assigned values (don't form bonds)

Metallic Character:
- Increases down a group
- Decreases across a period
- Metals: left side and center
- Nonmetals: right side
- Metalloids: staircase line dividing metals and nonmetals

Categories of Elements:

Metals:
- Left side and center of table
- Shiny, malleable, ductile
- Good conductors
- Lose electrons to form positive ions
- Examples: Iron, Aluminum, Gold

Nonmetals:
- Right side of table
- Dull appearance
- Poor conductors (insulators)
- Gain electrons to form negative ions
- Examples: Oxygen, Nitrogen, Sulfur

Metalloids (Semimetals):
- Boron, Silicon, Germanium, Arsenic, Antimony, Tellurium
- Properties between metals and nonmetals
- Semiconductors
- Used in electronics

Important Element Groups:

Lanthanides (Rare Earth Elements):
- Elements 57-71
- Shown separately below main table
- Soft, silvery metals
- Similar chemical properties

Actinides:
- Elements 89-103
- Shown below lanthanides
- All are radioactive
- Includes Uranium and Plutonium

Key Concepts:

Valence Electrons:
- Electrons in outermost shell
- Determine chemical properties
- Elements in same group have same number of valence electrons
- Maximum of 8 valence electrons (octet rule)

Isotopes:
- Atoms of same element with different numbers of neutrons
- Same atomic number, different mass number
- Example: Carbon-12, Carbon-13, Carbon-14

Ions:
- Atoms that have gained or lost electrons
- Cations: positive ions (lost electrons)
- Anions: negative ions (gained electrons)

Historical Development:
- Mendeleev arranged elements by atomic mass
- Predicted properties of undiscovered elements
- Modern table arranged by atomic number (Henry Moseley)
- Gaps in table led to discovery of new elements
