THE AMERICAN CIVIL WAR (1861-1865)

Causes of the Civil War:

Economic Differences:
- North: Industrial economy, factories, wage labor
- South: Agricultural economy, cotton plantations, slave labor
- Tariffs favored Northern manufacturing, hurt Southern exports
- North wanted high tariffs, South wanted free trade

Slavery:
- Central moral and economic issue
- South's economy dependent on enslaved labor
- North increasingly opposed slavery's expansion
- Abolitionist movement growing in North
- Missouri Compromise (1820) and Compromise of 1850 attempted to maintain balance

States' Rights vs Federal Power:
- South believed states could nullify federal laws
- Debate over federal government's authority
- Southern states claimed right to secede
- Question of sovereignty: state or federal?

Political Events Leading to War:

Kansas-Nebraska Act (1854):
- Allowed territories to decide slavery via popular sovereignty
- Led to violent conflict ("Bleeding Kansas")
- Undermined Missouri Compromise

Dred Scott Decision (1857):
- Supreme Court ruled enslaved people weren't citizens
- Declared Missouri Compromise unconstitutional
- Increased tensions between North and South

John Brown's Raid (1859):
- Attempted to start slave rebellion at Harpers Ferry
- Failed but terrified South
- Made him a martyr in the North

Election of Abraham Lincoln (1860):
- Republican candidate opposed slavery's expansion
- Won without a single Southern electoral vote
- South saw this as threat to their way of life
- Triggered secession

Secession:
- South Carolina seceded December 1860
- Ten more states followed by June 1861
- Confederate States of America formed February 1861
- Jefferson Davis elected Confederate President

Major Battles:

Fort Sumter (April 1861):
- First battle of the war
- Confederate forces fired on Union fort
- No casualties but marked beginning of war

First Battle of Bull Run (July 1861):
- First major land battle
- Confederate victory
- Showed war would be long and bloody

Battle of Antietam (September 1862):
- Bloodiest single day in American history
- 23,000 casualties
- Strategic Union victory
- Led to Emancipation Proclamation

Battle of Gettysburg (July 1863):
- Turning point of the war
- Union victory ended Lee's invasion of North
- Gettysburg Address delivered by Lincoln

Siege of Vicksburg (July 1863):
- Union gained control of Mississippi River
- Split Confederacy in two
- Major strategic victory

Sherman's March to the Sea (1864):
- Total war strategy
- Destroyed Southern infrastructure
- Broke Confederate morale

Appomattox Court House (April 1865):
- Lee surrendered to Grant
- Effectively ended the war

Key Figures:

Abraham Lincoln:
- 16th President
- Preserved the Union
- Issued Emancipation Proclamation
- Assassinated April 14, 1865

Ulysses S. Grant:
- Union general
- Commander of all Union armies
- Later became 18th President

Robert E. Lee:
- Confederate general
- Commander of Army of Northern Virginia
- Skilled military tactician

Jefferson Davis:
- President of Confederate States
- Former U.S. Senator from Mississippi

Emancipation Proclamation (January 1, 1863):
- Declared slaves in rebel states free
- Did not apply to border states
- Changed war's purpose to include ending slavery
- Allowed Black soldiers to join Union army

Results of the War:
- 620,000-750,000 deaths
- Union preserved
- Slavery abolished (13th Amendment, 1865)
- South's economy devastated
- Federal government's power strengthened
- Reconstruction period began (1865-1877)

Amendments After the War:
- 13th Amendment: Abolished slavery
- 14th Amendment: Granted citizenship to former slaves
- 15th Amendment: Gave Black men right to vote
