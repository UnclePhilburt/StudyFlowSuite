ANCIENT ROME - WORLD HISTORY STUDY GUIDE

Timeline of Roman History:

753 BCE - Founding of Rome (legendary)
509 BCE - Roman Republic established
264-146 BCE - Punic Wars
44 BCE - Julius Caesar assassinated
27 BCE - Roman Empire begins (Augustus)
476 CE - Western Roman Empire falls
1453 CE - Eastern Roman (Byzantine) Empire falls

Three Periods of Roman History:

1. Roman Kingdom (753-509 BCE)
- Legendary founding by Romulus
- Ruled by kings
- Seven kings according to tradition
- Last king: Tarquin the Proud (overthrown)

2. Roman Republic (509-27 BCE)
- Representative government
- Senate and assemblies
- Two consuls elected annually
- Period of expansion
- Ended with civil wars

3. Roman Empire (27 BCE-476 CE)
- Rule by emperors
- Started with Augustus
- Height of Roman power and culture
- Gradual decline and fall

Geography:

Location:
- Italian Peninsula in Mediterranean
- Center of Mediterranean world
- Seven hills of Rome
- Tiber River
- Strategic location for trade

Expansion:
- Started as small city-state
- Conquered entire Italian Peninsula
- Expanded around Mediterranean Sea
- At height: Britain to Mesopotamia, North Africa to Rhine River
- "Mare Nostrum" (Our Sea) - Mediterranean

Government of the Republic:

Consuls:
- Two chief executives
- Elected for one-year terms
- Commanded army
- Could veto each other

Senate:
- 300 members (patricians)
- Advised consuls
- Controlled foreign policy and finances
- Most powerful body in Republic

Assemblies:
- Centuriate Assembly: voted on laws, elected consuls
- Tribal Assembly: elected lower officials
- Plebeian Assembly: elected tribunes

Tribunes:
- Protected plebeians' rights
- Could veto Senate decisions
- Proposed laws

Social Classes:

Patricians:
- Wealthy landowners
- Aristocratic class
- Held most political power
- About 5-10% of population

Plebeians:
- Common people
- Farmers, artisans, merchants
- Initially excluded from government
- Gained rights through Conflict of Orders

Slaves:
- Prisoners of war, debtors
- No rights
- Could be freed (become freedmen)
- Essential to Roman economy

Major Conflicts and Expansions:

Punic Wars (264-146 BCE):
Three wars with Carthage for Mediterranean dominance

First Punic War (264-241 BCE):
- Naval war
- Rome gained Sicily
- Rome became naval power

Second Punic War (218-201 BCE):
- Hannibal crossed Alps with elephants
- Nearly defeated Rome
- Battle of Cannae: worst Roman defeat
- Scipio Africanus defeated Hannibal at Zama
- Rome gained Spain

Third Punic War (149-146 BCE):
- Rome destroyed Carthage completely
- "Carthago delenda est" (Carthage must be destroyed)
- Rome dominated western Mediterranean

Other Conquests:
- Macedonian Wars: conquered Greece
- Conquest of Gaul (France) by Julius Caesar
- Expansion into Asia Minor, Syria, Egypt

End of the Republic - Civil Wars:

First Triumvirate (60 BCE):
- Julius Caesar, Pompey, Crassus
- Informal political alliance
- Shared power

Julius Caesar (100-44 BCE):
- Conquered Gaul
- Crossed Rubicon River (49 BCE) - "the die is cast"
- Civil war with Pompey
- Became dictator
- Assassinated on Ides of March (March 15, 44 BCE)
- Senators feared he'd become king

Second Triumvirate (43 BCE):
- Octavian (Augustus), Mark Antony, Lepidus
- Official political alliance
- Hunted Caesar's assassins

Battle of Actium (31 BCE):
- Octavian defeated Antony and Cleopatra
- End of Republic
- Octavian became sole ruler

The Roman Empire:

Augustus (27 BCE - 14 CE):
- First emperor
- Originally named Octavian
- Senate gave him title "Augustus" (revered one)
- Pax Romana (Roman Peace) began
- Expanded empire's borders
- Reformed government, army, tax system
- Great patron of arts

Pax Romana (27 BCE - 180 CE):
- 200 years of relative peace
- Trade flourished
- Arts and architecture thrived
- Roman law spread
- Roads connected empire
- Stable government

Famous Emperors:

Tiberius (14-37 CE): Continued Augustus's policies
Caligula (37-41 CE): Considered mad/tyrannical
Claudius (41-54 CE): Conquered Britain
Nero (54-68 CE): Persecuted Christians, blamed for Rome fire
Vespasian (69-79 CE): Built Colosseum
Trajan (98-117 CE): Empire reached maximum size
Hadrian (117-138 CE): Built Hadrian's Wall in Britain
Marcus Aurelius (161-180 CE): Philosopher-emperor

Roman Achievements:

Architecture:
- Arches and domes
- Aqueducts (water supply systems)
- Colosseum (amphitheater for gladiator games)
- Pantheon (temple with huge dome)
- Roman roads (50,000 miles)
- Concrete construction

Law:
- Twelve Tables: first written Roman laws
- Innocent until proven guilty
- Right to face accuser
- Foundation of Western legal systems
- Civil law vs criminal law

Language:
- Latin: language of Rome
- Basis for Romance languages (Italian, French, Spanish, Portuguese, Romanian)
- Used in science, law, medicine

Engineering:
- Aqueducts brought water to cities
- Road system connected empire
- Bridges, tunnels
- Sewage systems

Literature:
- Virgil: Aeneid (epic poem)
- Ovid: Metamorphoses
- Cicero: speeches and philosophy
- Livy: History of Rome

Military:
- Legions: professional soldiers
- 5,000-6,000 men per legion
- Disciplined and organized
- Conquered most of known world

Religion:

Traditional Roman Religion:
- Polytheistic (many gods)
- Borrowed from Greek mythology
- Jupiter (Zeus), Juno (Hera), Mars (Ares), etc.
- Emperor worship
- Household gods (Lares and Penates)

Christianity:
- Jesus born during reign of Augustus
- Spread throughout empire
- Initially persecuted
- Constantine converted (312 CE)
- Edict of Milan (313 CE): legalized Christianity
- Theodosius made it official religion (380 CE)

Decline and Fall:

Causes of Decline:
- Political instability (frequent emperor changes)
- Economic troubles (inflation, heavy taxes)
- Military problems (mercenary armies, barbarian invasions)
- Moral decay (according to some historians)
- Division of empire (East and West, 285 CE)
- Rise of Christianity (shifted focus from earthly to spiritual)
- Large territory difficult to defend

Fall of Western Empire:
- 410 CE: Visigoths sacked Rome
- 455 CE: Vandals sacked Rome
- 476 CE: Last Western emperor (Romulus Augustulus) deposed
- Germanic tribes took over western provinces

Eastern Empire (Byzantine):
- Continued until 1453
- Capital: Constantinople
- Preserved Roman and Greek culture
- Fell to Ottoman Turks

Legacy:
- Law and government systems
- Latin language and literature
- Architecture and engineering
- Calendar (Julian, later Gregorian)
- Alphabet
- Christianity as major religion
- Concept of citizenship
- Foundation of Western civilization
